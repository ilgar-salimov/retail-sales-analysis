The queries in this project use a combination of basic and advanced SQL techniques to answer business questions.

• Filtering queries (WHERE, DISTINCT) are used to retrieve specific records such as high-priced products and unique customer countries. 

• Join operations (INNER JOIN, LEFT JOIN) combine multiple tables to build complete datasets and identify relationships, such as linking orders with customers, products, and suppliers.

• Aggregation queries (SUM, COUNT, GROUP BY) are used to measure performance metrics like total revenue, number of orders, and product/category statistics. 

• Sorting and limiting (ORDER BY, LIMIT) help identify top results such as best customers or top-performing employees. 

• Date-based analysis uses a date dimension table to analyze trends over time, including monthly revenue and weekday vs weekend comparisons. 

• Filtering aggregated data (HAVING) is applied to conditions on grouped results. 

• Window functions (RANK ()) are used to rank customers by total spending for segmentation. 

• CTEs and subqueries are used to structure complex queries into readable and reusable steps.

Overall, the queries demonstrate the use of SQL for data extraction, transformation, aggregation, and analytical reporting.
